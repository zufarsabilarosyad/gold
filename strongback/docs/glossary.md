# Glossary

**Application for payment** — the monthly request: a summary page and a
continuation sheet.  Prepared by the payee, certified by the payer or their
architect, and paid some days later.

**Back-charge** — a cost the payer incurred on the payee's behalf, deducted
from the payee's application.  Distinct from an offset: a back-charge pays for
work, an offset withholds against a risk.

**Balance to finish** — contract sum less what has been earned.  With retainage
(line 9 of the summary) or without it, depending on who is asking.

**Change order** — a priced change to the contract.  Proposed, directed,
approved, executed, rejected or void; which of those may be billed is a
contract term.

**Conditional waiver** — a lien waiver that takes effect only when the payment
it names actually clears.  An unconditional one takes effect on signature,
which is why signing one against an uncleared cheque is dangerous.

**Continuation sheet** — the line-by-line detail behind an application:
scheduled value, previous, this period, stored, total, percent, balance and
retainage.

**Contract sum to date** — the original contract sum plus the change orders
that are billable at the date in question.

**Cost to complete** — the forecast cost still to be spent on a line or a job.

**Directed work** — work the owner has instructed but has not executed as a
change order.  It has been done; whether it may be billed depends on the
contract.

**Liquidated damages** — a stated amount per day of late completion, usually
capped.  Once assessed they are gone, unlike a reversible offset.

**Over-billing** — billing that runs ahead of earned revenue.  Normal early in
a job, dangerous late in one.

**Pay-if-paid** — a condition precedent: if the owner never pays, the
subcontractor is never owed.  Not enforceable everywhere.

**Pay-when-paid** — a timing clause: the money is owed, and payment may wait on
the owner for a reasonable time.

**Punchlist** — the remaining minor work at substantial completion.  Its value
often sets the retainage holdback, at one and a half or two times.

**Retainage** — the share of earned value the payer holds back until the payee
finishes.  Also called retention.

**Schedule of values** — the contract's own breakdown of its price into billing
lines.  Every dollar is billed against one of them.

**Stored materials** — material delivered and paid for but not yet installed.

**Substantial completion** — the point at which the owner can occupy or use the
work.  Usually triggers a large release of retainage.

**Unit-price line** — a line billed per measured unit against an estimated
quantity, rather than as a percentage of a lump sum.

**Work in progress (WIP)** — the report comparing earned revenue with billing,
contract by contract.
